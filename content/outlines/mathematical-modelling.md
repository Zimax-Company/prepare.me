Detailed Course Curriculum
Level 1: Foundations of Mathematical Modelling
Objective: Understand the philosophy, principles and processes involved in converting real-world problems into mathematical models.
Module	Topics
1.1	Introduction to mathematical modelling
1.2	History and evolution of mathematical modelling
1.3	Mathematics as a language for describing reality
1.4	Types of mathematical models
1.5	Deterministic vs stochastic models
1.6	Static vs dynamic models
1.7	Linear vs nonlinear models
1.8	Continuous vs discrete models
1.9	Variables, parameters and constants
1.10	Model assumptions and simplifications
1.11	Dimensional analysis and units
1.12	Model formulation and mathematical abstraction
1.13	Model calibration and validation
1.14	Limitations and interpretation of mathematical models

Expected competencies:
- Identify real-world problems suitable for mathematical modelling.
- Translate practical problems into mathematical language.
- Identify dependent and independent variables.
- Formulate appropriate assumptions.
- Understand the strengths and limitations of different model types.
Assessment: Develop mathematical models for simple real-world problems.

Module	Topics

2.11	Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
2.12	Probability theory
2.13	Descriptive and inferential statistics
2.14	Correlation and regression
2.15	Basic optimisation techniques

Expected competencies:
- Select appropriate mathematical functions for modelling.
- Apply calculus to analyse changing quantities.
- Use matrices to represent systems of equations.
- Apply probability and statistics to uncertain phenomena.
- Understand the mathematical structures underlying different models.
Assessment: Mathematical exercises and construction of simple models using algebra, calculus and statistics.

Expected competencies:
- Construct mathematical models from business and economic problems.
- Formulate and solve systems of equations.
- Develop optimisation models.
- Identify equilibrium conditions.
- Analyse how changes in model parameters affect outcomes.
Practical applications:
- Profit maximisation.
- Workforce allocation.
- Resource optimisation.
- Production planning.
- Budget optimisation.
- Pricing and demand analysis.
Assessment: Build and solve a deterministic optimisation model for a real-world problem.

Level 4: Dynamic Models and Differential Equations
Objective: Understand systems that evolve or change over time.

This is one of the most important levels because many real-world phenomena are dynamic rather than static.
Module	Topics
4.1	Introduction to dynamic systems
4.2	Difference equations
4.3	Ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
4.4	First-order differential equations
4.5	Second-order differential equations

4.7	Initial value problems

4.11	Population dynamics
4.12	Predator-prey models
4.13	Compartmental models
4.14	Equilibrium and steady-state analysis
4.15	Stability analysis
4.16	Phase-plane analysis
4.17	Introduction to partial differential equations

Expected competencies:
- Represent time-dependent systems using differential equations.
- Analyse growth and decay.
- Understand equilibrium and stability.
- Model interacting populations and other dynamic systems.
- Interpret the long-term behaviour of dynamic models.
Practical applications:
- Population growth.
- Disease transmission.
- Employee attrition and workforce growth.
- Financial asset growth.
- Inventory dynamics.
- Product adoption and market penetration.
Assessment: Formulate and analyse a dynamic model using differential equations.

- Financial market uncertainty.

- Employee absenteeism.

Level 6: Computational and Numerical Modelling
Objective: Learn how to implement mathematical models using computational tools and numerical methods.

Mathematical models are often too complex to solve analytically. Computational modelling allows us to approximate solutions and simulate complex systems.
Module	Topics
6.1	Introduction to computational modelling
6.2	Python for mathematical modelling
6.3	MATLAB or equivalent numerical computing environments
6.4	Numerical approximation
6.5	Numerical solutions of algebraic equations
6.6	Numerical differentiation and integration
6.7	Numerical solutions of differential equations
6.8	Euler's method
6.9	Runge-Kutta methods
6.10	Numerical optimisation
6.11	Matrix computation
6.12	Data visualisation
6.13	Computational simulation
6.14	Agent-based modelling
6.15	Model implementation and debugging
6.16	Computational model verification

Expected competencies:
- Translate mathematical equations into executable programs.
- Implement models using Python.
- Solve mathematical problems numerically.
- Simulate real-world systems.
- Visualise model outputs.
- Identify computational errors and numerical limitations.
Recommended tools:
- Python (NumPy, SciPy, SymPy).
- Jupyter Notebook.
- MATLAB.
- R.
- Excel for introductory modelling.
Assessment: Build a computational simulation of a real-world system.

Final assessment: Complete a mathematical modelling research project involving problem formulation, mathematical derivation, computational implementation, validation and presentation.
