Detailed Course Curriculum
Level 1: Mathematical Foundations and Proof Techniques
Objective: Develop the mathematical language and logical reasoning required to understand and construct proofs.
Module	Topics
1.1	Introduction to mathematical reasoning
1.2	Propositional logic and logical operators
1.3	Quantifiers: For all (∀) and There exists (∃)
1.4	Set theory and set operations
1.5	Relations and equivalence relations
1.6	Functions: injective, surjective and bijective
1.7	Mathematical induction
1.8	Direct proofs
1.9	Proof by contradiction
1.10	Proof by contrapositive
1.11	Counterexamples and disproofs

Expected competencies:
- Understand mathematical statements and their logical structure.
- Construct valid mathematical proofs.
- Work confidently with sets and functions.
- Understand the difference between intuition and mathematical proof.
Assessment: Proof-writing exercises and logical reasoning problems.

Level 2: The Real Number System
Objective: Understand the mathematical structure and fundamental properties of real numbers.
Module	Topics
2.1	Natural, integer, rational and irrational numbers
2.2	Properties of real numbers
2.3	Field axioms
2.4	Order properties
2.5	Absolute values and inequalities
2.6	Intervals and neighbourhoods
2.7	Bounded and unbounded sets
2.8	Upper and lower bounds
2.9	Supremum and infimum
2.10	The completeness property of real numbers
2.11	Archimedean property
2.12	Density of rational and irrational numbers

Expected competencies:
- Explain why the real number system is complete.
- Determine the supremum and infimum of sets.
- Prove inequalities using real number properties.
- Distinguish between rational and irrational numbers.
Assessment: Exercises involving bounds, completeness and proofs.

Level 3: Sequences and Limits
Objective: Understand convergence and the behaviour of infinite sequences.
Module	Topics
3.1	Introduction to sequences
3.2	Bounded and unbounded sequences
3.3	Convergent and divergent sequences
3.4	The epsilon-N definition of convergence
3.5	Uniqueness of limits
3.6	Limit laws
3.7	Monotone sequences
3.8	Monotone convergence theorem
3.9	Subsequences
3.10	Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem
3.11	Cauchy sequences
3.12	Cauchy convergence criterion
3.13	Limit superior and limit inferior

Expected competencies:
- Determine whether a sequence converges.
- Prove convergence using formal definitions.
- Understand the relationship between boundedness and convergence.
- Apply important convergence theorems.
Assessment: Sequence convergence proofs and problem-solving exercises.

Level 4: Infinite Series
Objective: Study infinite sums and the conditions under which they converge.
Module	Topics
4.1	Introduction to infinite series
4.2	Partial sums
4.3	Convergence and divergence of series
4.4	Necessary condition for convergence
4.5	Geometric series
4.6	Harmonic series
4.7	Comparison test
4.8	Limit comparison test
4.9	Ratio test
4.10	Root test
4.11	Alternating series test
4.12	Absolute and conditional convergence
4.13	Rearrangement of series
4.14	Power series
4.15	Radius and interval of convergence

Expected competencies:
- Determine whether an infinite series converges or diverges.
- Select the appropriate convergence test.
- Understand absolute and conditional convergence.
- Determine the radius of convergence of power series.
Assessment: Series convergence problems and proof-based assignments.

Level 5: Limits and Continuity of Functions
Objective: Develop a rigorous understanding of function limits and continuity.
Module	Topics
5.1	Limits of real-valued functions
5.2	Epsilon-delta definition of limits
5.3	One-sided limits
5.4	Limit laws
5.5	Infinite limits and limits at infinity
5.6	Continuity at a point
5.7	Continuity on an interval
5.8	Types of discontinuities
5.9	Intermediate Value Theorem
5.10	Extreme Value Theorem
5.11	Uniform continuity
5.12	Heine-Cantor theorem
5.13	Continuous functions on compact intervals

Expected competencies:
- Prove function limits using epsilon-delta arguments.
- Determine continuity and discontinuity.
- Understand the relationship between continuity and compactness.
- Apply the major continuity theorems.
Assessment: Formal limit proofs and continuity problems.

Level 6: Differentiation
Objective: Understand differentiation from a rigorous mathematical perspective.
Module	Topics
6.1	Introduction to derivatives
6.2	Derivative from first principles
6.3	Differentiability and continuity
6.4	Algebra of derivatives
6.5	Chain rule
6.6	Rolle's theorem
6.7	Mean Value Theorem
6.8	Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem
6.9	Applications of the Mean Value Theorem
6.10	Taylor's theorem
6.11	Higher-order derivatives
6.12	Convexity and concavity
6.13	L'Hôpital's rule and its justification

Expected competencies:
- Understand the formal definition of differentiation.
- Prove important differentiation theorems.
- Apply the Mean Value Theorem.
- Understand how differentiability relates to continuity.
Assessment: Derivative proofs, theorem applications and analytical problems.

Level 7: Riemann Integration
Objective: Establish the theoretical foundation of integration and its relationship with differentiation.
Module	Topics
7.1	Introduction to integration
7.2	Partitions of intervals
7.3	Upper and lower sums
7.4	Upper and lower integrals
7.5	Riemann integrability
7.6	Integrability of continuous functions
7.7	Integrability of monotone functions
7.8	Properties of the Riemann integral
7.9	Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
7.10	Mean Value Theorem for integrals
7.11	Improper integrals
7.12	Integrability and sets of measure zero (introductory)

Expected competencies:
- Explain integration using partitions and Riemann sums.
- Determine whether functions are Riemann integrable.
- Prove important integration results.
- Understand the connection between differentiation and integration.
Assessment: Integrability proofs and theoretical integration problems.

Level 8: Advanced Real Analysis
Objective: Extend classical real analysis into more abstract mathematical structures and advanced convergence concepts.
Module	Topics
8.1	Introduction to metric spaces
8.2	Open and closed sets
8.3	Interior, closure and boundary
8.4	Compactness
8.5	Connectedness
8.6	Completeness of metric spaces
8.7	Sequences and convergence in metric spaces
8.8	Pointwise convergence of functions
8.9	Uniform convergence
8.10	Uniform convergence and continuity
8.11	Uniform convergence and integration
8.12	Equicontinuity
8.13	Arzelà-Ascoli theorem (introduction)
8.14	Introduction to Lebesgue measure
8.15	Measurable functions
8.16	Introduction to Lebesgue integration

Expected competencies:
- Work with abstract metric spaces.
- Understand compactness and completeness.
- Distinguish pointwise from uniform convergence.
- Understand the motivation and foundations of Lebesgue integration.
Assessment: Advanced proof-writing, theorem applications and research-oriented exercises.
